# Portfolio Communication & Positioning Guide

## Purpose

This document defines how the portfolio should communicate Santam's professional identity.

The goal is NOT to redesign the portfolio from scratch.

The goal is to improve the communication, copy, hierarchy, and positioning so that the portfolio accurately represents the work Santam currently does and supports future opportunities.

The existing visual personality and character of the portfolio should be preserved wherever possible.

---

# Who I Am

My official job title is currently **Chief of Staff** at a small consulting and technology company.

However, this title should not be interpreted in the traditional corporate sense.

The company has approximately five employees, and I do not manage a team or perform the typical executive Chief of Staff responsibilities.

My actual work is much broader and more hands-on.

I work across:

- Websites and frontend development
- Digital products and internal tools
- Technology research
- Client documentation
- Requirement gathering
- Client communication
- Researching unfamiliar industries and problems
- Preparing proposals and tender applications
- Digital marketing experiments
- Meta advertising
- Exploring AI tools and workflows
- Finding and evaluating technology solutions
- Helping move ideas from vague requirements toward practical deliverables

I often work on problems where there is no predefined process.

My role is frequently to understand the problem, research possible approaches, identify useful tools or technologies, and help move the work toward a practical solution.

# Core Professional Positioning

The portfolio should NOT position me only as:

- A Frontend Developer
- A React Developer
- A Web Designer
- A Freelancer who only builds websites
- A traditional Chief of Staff
- A senior consultant or strategy consultant

These descriptions are either too narrow or inaccurate.

Instead, the portfolio should position me as someone working at the intersection of:

**Technology + Digital Solutions + Research + Client Delivery + Problem Solving**

The central idea should be:

> I help turn messy problems and ideas into practical digital solutions.

A visitor should understand that I can work across different parts of a project.

This includes understanding the problem, researching options, working with clients, creating documentation, selecting or understanding technology, and contributing to the actual implementation.

# What I Do

The portfolio should communicate four connected areas of work.

These should not feel like unrelated services.

They should communicate different parts of the same ability: understanding problems and helping build practical solutions.

---

## 1. Websites & Digital Products

I build websites, dashboards and digital tools.

However, the communication should go beyond:

> I build beautiful websites.

Instead:

> I work on websites and digital products with an eye on both the user and the problem they are meant to solve.

Example description:

> From public-facing websites to dashboards and internal tools, I work through the problem, user journey and implementation—not just the visual layer.

---

## 2. Research & Solution Design

This is an important part of my actual work.

I frequently work with unfamiliar topics, industries and technologies.

My process often involves:

- Understanding the problem
- Researching the subject
- Comparing possible approaches
- Identifying useful tools or technologies
- Structuring information
- Creating documentation or recommendations

Suggested communication:

> Not every problem starts with a clear brief. Sometimes the work is figuring out what the actual problem is, what options exist and what makes sense to do next.

Alternative:

> I enjoy working through unfamiliar problems—researching the landscape, comparing approaches and turning scattered information into something people can actually act on.

---

## 3. Client Projects & Delivery

The portfolio should communicate that I have experience working with real clients.

This includes:

- Gathering inputs
- Understanding requirements
- Asking the right questions
- Clarifying expectations
- Creating documentation
- Translating ideas into technical or digital work
- Helping move projects toward delivery

Suggested copy:

> I work directly with clients to understand what they need, gather the right inputs and help move projects from vague ideas to clear deliverables.

Alternative:

> Good projects usually start before anything is built. I work with clients to understand the problem, clarify requirements and create a clearer path toward delivery.

---

## 4. Technology & AI Workflows

AI should not be presented as a gimmick.

The portfolio should communicate practical use.

I use tools such as AI systems to:

- Research faster
- Explore unfamiliar topics
- Draft and structure documentation
- Understand technical concepts
- Experiment with solutions
- Reduce repetitive work
- Assist development and problem solving

Suggested communication:

> I use modern tools, including AI, to research faster, explore solutions and reduce repetitive work. The tool matters less than whether the result is actually useful.

# How I Think

This section should communicate my approach to unfamiliar problems.

The important message is:

**I do not start with a tool. I start with the problem.**

Suggested section:

## I start with the problem, not the tool.

> A website, dashboard, automation or AI workflow is only useful if it solves the right problem. I try to understand what is actually happening before deciding what should be built.

Additional ideas:

### Comfortable with unfamiliar territory

> I don't need to know everything before starting. I'm comfortable researching unfamiliar subjects, reading documentation and working my way toward an answer.

### Curious by default

> If I don't understand something, I usually end up going down a rabbit hole until I do.

### Practical over complicated

> I like technology, but I don't think every problem needs more technology. Sometimes the best solution is the simplest one that actually works.

The existing personality of lines such as:

> "I read documentation the way other people read the news."

can be preserved.

The portfolio should feel curious and human, not like a corporate consulting website.

# Project Case Studies

Projects should not only communicate:

- What was built
- Which technologies were used

Each project should demonstrate how I think and work.

Use this structure wherever possible.

---

## The Context

What was the situation?

What kind of business, user or problem was involved?

---

## The Problem

What needed to be solved?

Avoid vague descriptions such as:

> The client needed a modern website.

Instead explain:

> Information was difficult to navigate, the existing experience did not clearly communicate the offering, and users needed a simpler path to find relevant information.

---

## My Role

Clearly explain what I personally contributed.

Possible examples:

- Research
- Requirement gathering
- Client communication
- Information architecture
- Frontend development
- Design collaboration
- Technical implementation
- Workflow planning

Avoid making it sound like I did everything unless that was genuinely the case.

---

## The Approach

Explain how the work was approached.

For example:

> I started by understanding the available information and the user journey, then structured the content and translated the requirements into the final experience.

Or:

> The initial problem was unclear, so the first step was understanding how the existing workflow operated before deciding what needed to be built.

---

## What Was Delivered

Describe the actual result.

Examples:

- Website
- Dashboard
- Internal tool
- Booking experience
- Information system
- Workflow
- Prototype

---

## Outcome

Where possible, explain what improved.

Do not invent metrics.

If hard numbers are unavailable, describe the practical result.

Examples:

> Created a clearer way to manage bookings and availability.

> Turned a scattered set of requirements into a structured digital experience.

> Provided a single interface for information that was previously handled across multiple places.
